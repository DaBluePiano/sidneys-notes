# Chapter 2 — Numbers and Surds

> **Scope:** set notation, approximations, surd arithmetic and rationalisation.

---

## Contents

- Number sets: $\mathbb{N},\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R}$
- Approximations and significant figures

---

## Essentials

### Number Sets

- $\mathbb{N}$: whole numbers 0,1,2,...
- $\mathbb{Z}$: integers (positive, negative, zero)
- $\mathbb{Q}$: rationals (fractions)
- $\mathbb{R}$: reals (includes irrationals like $\sqrt2,\pi$)

### Approximations

- Use scientific notation to show significant figures: e.g. $3.20\times10^3$.
- Round carefully: to $n$ decimal places look at $(n+1)^\text{th}$ digit; indicate approximation with $\approx$.

### Surds (exact radicals)

- Surd = expression left in root form (e.g. $\sqrt{3}$).
- Simplify by extracting perfect powers:
$$
\sqrt{72}=\sqrt{36\cdot2}=6\sqrt2.
$$
- Multiply/divide under radicals when nonnegative:
$$
\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\,\sqrt b,\quad \frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b}=\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}.
$$

### Rationalising Denominators

- Remove surds from denominator by multiplying by conjugate where needed:
$$
\frac{1}{\sqrt a}=\frac{\sqrt a}{a},\qquad
\frac{1}{\sqrt p+\sqrt q}=\frac{\sqrt p-\sqrt q}{p-q}.
$$
- After rationalising, simplify and state any excluded values.

---

## Study Tips

- Keep answers in exact surd form when algebraic work follows; convert to decimals only when required.
- Practice recognising factorisation that helps simplify surds quickly.
