## Key Geographical Skills

These skills appear in multiple choice and short answers. Practice interpreting them using real examples.

### Maps

- **Latitude and Longitude**: Latitude runs north-south (equator at 0°), measuring up to 90° N/S. Longitude runs east-west (Prime Meridian at 0°), measuring up to 180° E/W. Main lines: Equator, Tropics of Cancer/Capricorn, Arctic/Antarctic Circles, Prime Meridian, International Date Line.
- **Scale and Distance**: Types include linear (straight line with divisions), ratio (e.g., 1:50,000 means 1 cm = 500 m), and statement (e.g., "1 cm represents 1 km"). Use to calculate distances.
- **Area and Grid References**: Grid references use Eastings (horizontal, first) then Northings (vertical). Area references identify larger zones.
- **Map Types**:
    - Political: Shows country/state borders and capitals.
    - Topographic: Shows relief (elevation) with contours.
    - Choropleth: Uses shading or colors to represent data density (e.g., population per area).

### Graphs and Statistics

- **Types**:
    - Pie graphs: Show proportions/percentages in a circle (e.g., land use breakdown).
    - Column graphs: Compare categories using bars (e.g., crop yields by biome).
    - Line graphs: Show trends over time (e.g., temperature changes).
    - Scatter graphs: Show relationships between two variables (e.g., rainfall vs. crop yield).
    - Climate graphs: Combined column (precipitation) and line (temperature). Summer months have higher temperatures (peak in line); winter has lower. Calculate temperature range: Maximum temp - minimum temp.

### Visual Representations

- **Photographs**: Including aerial types.
    - Ground level: Eye-level view for detail.
    - Oblique aerial: Angled from air, shows perspective.
    - Vertical aerial: Straight down, like a map.
    - Satellite: From space, covers large areas (e.g., for monitoring deforestation).

## Key Content Areas

### Sustainable Biomes

- **Definition**: Biomes are large-scale ecosystems defined by climate, vegetation, and animals (e.g., Alpine, Tundra, Polar/Arctic/Antarctic, Marine, Desert, Temperate Forest, Rainforest, Taiga, Savanna).
- **Spatial Distribution**: Biomes are located based on latitude, climate, soils, and vegetation. E.g., Rainforests near equator (high rain, warm), deserts at 30° N/S (dry), tundra near poles (cold).

- **Physical Characteristics**: Climate (temp/rainfall) influences soils (e.g., fertile in grasslands) and vegetation (e.g., sparse in deserts).
- **Biomes Used for Production**: Grasslands and temperate forests for food (crops/livestock), fibres (cotton), industrial materials (timber). Productivity impacted by climate (e.g., drought reduces yields), soils (nutrient-rich boost growth), vegetation (natural cover affects farming suitability).

- **Human Alterations**: To produce food/materials – vegetation removal (clearing for fields), agriculture (crop planting), land terracing (for slopes), irrigation (water channels), mining (resource extraction).

- **Environmental Impacts**: Habitat/biodiversity loss (species extinction), water pollution (from chemicals), salinity (soil salt buildup from irrigation).
- **Sustainable Strategies**: Crop rotation (maintain soil health), reforestation (plant trees), drip irrigation (reduce water waste), regulations (protected areas), organic farming (no chemicals).

### Biomes Produce Food

- **Net Primary Productivity (NPP)**: The rate at which plants produce usable biomass after accounting for respiration – measures biome productivity for food.
- **Factors Influencing Agricultural Yields**:

    |Category|Factors|Examples|

    |---|---|---|

    |Environmental|Temperature, water availability, soil, topography|Warm temps for crops; fertile soil increases yields; steep topography limits farming.|

    |Economic|Global trade, commercialisation of agriculture|Exports drive production; large-scale farms for efficiency.|

    |Technological|Innovations in farming|Machinery, GMOs, fertilizers boost output.|

### Challenges to Food Production

- **Environmental Challenges**:
    - Water scarcity: Limits plant growth as crops need water; also competes with human needs.
    - Pollution: Non-biodegradable substances (e.g., pesticides) harm soil/plants.
    - Land degradation: Erosion/salinity makes land unusable for crops.
    - Competing land uses: Urban expansion or biofuels reduce farmland.
- **Climate Change Impact**: Warmer temps, droughts, floods make food harder to grow; shifts suitable areas, reduces yields in vulnerable biomes.

### Food Security

- **Definition**: Reliable access to sufficient, nutritious food.
- **Capacity of Biomes**: Biomes can sustain food if managed, but overexploitation risks depletion.
- **Future Demand**: Rising population (e.g., to 10 billion by 2050) increases demand, straining resources.
- **Sustainable Practices**: Precision farming (targeted resources), conservation tillage (reduce erosion), diversified crops.
- **Australia's Contribution**: Major exporter (wheat, beef); shares tech like drought-resistant crops; aids global security via trade/aid.

### Changing Places: Causes and Consequences of Urbanisation

- **Definition**: Growth of cities as people move from rural to urban areas.
- **Causes**: Economic opportunities (jobs), better services (education/health), rural push factors (poverty, mechanised farming), natural population increase.
- **Consequences**:
    - Positive: Economic growth, innovation, cultural diversity.
    - Negative: Overcrowding, slums, pollution, strain on infrastructure, loss of farmland.
    - Perspectives: Governments see growth potential; residents may face inequality; organisations push sustainable urban planning.
    - Sustainability: Green spaces, public transport, waste management.
