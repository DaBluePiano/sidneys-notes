# Skills

## Maps

Most world maps are either:

- **Political** - Colours different countries or political regions with different colours
- **Physical** - Uses colours and shading to show physical features like biomes and landforms
- **Landure** - A choropleth map that shows agricultural, industrial and urban uses of land

**Topographic** - These maps combine the main features of both physical and political maps. They show both human and natural features. They also show height and relief of land by using contour lines.

![[image-3.png|500x375]]

**Choropleth** - Choropleth maps show a feature of an area by shading different sections. For example, areas with high population might be dark blue and areas with low population might be light blue.

## Area and Grid References

Many maps, most usually topographic maps are covered in a grid that allows you to locate places on the map.

**Eastings**
	The grid has lines that run vertically. These are called **Eastings** because their number increases in an **easterly** direction.
	The number can start with any value but will always be a **two digit** number.
**Northings**
	The grid has lines that run **horizontally**. These are called **Northings** because their number increases in a northerly direction.
	The number can start with any value but will always be a **two digit** number.

When you give an Area or Grid reference, rememeber to put **Eastings** before **Northings** because that is *alphabetical order*

### Area Reference

An area reference describes a whole grid square on the map by naming the line that comes at the start of the grid square for **Eastings** and for **Northings**. This gives you a **4 digit** Area Reference.

eg.

AR 0643

### Grid Reference

A grid reference describes a specific point in a grid square by **imagining** a decimal point within the square. This gives you a **6 digit** grid reference. You don't need to write the decimal point because all Geographers know that its there

eg.

GR 06**8**43**5**

## Latitude and Longitude

	Longitude lines tell you **east** and **west**

When giving coordinates, **latitude** comes first because it comes first in the alphabet.

## Photographs
### Ground Level
**Advantages**: Great detail can be seen. The relative height of nearby objects is very clear.
**Disadvantages**: You can't see the spatial relationship between objects in a large area (ie. where they are in relation to each other).
### Oblique Aerial
**Advantages**: You can see both the relationship of the height and location between major objects. An oblique photograph is a good compromise between ground level and vertical aerial as it shows features of both.

**Disadvantages**: The height difference between objects is not clear and the distance between objects is not clear.
### Vertical Aerial

**Advantages**: The spatial relationship between objects is very clear. Vertical aerial photos are good for making maps.

**Disadvantages**: You can’t see the relative height between objects. There is less detail.

### Satellite

**Advantages**: These are the same as vertical aerial photographs: the spatial relationship between objects is very clear. Satellite photos are good for making maps.

**Disadvantages**: You can’t see the relative height between objects. There is less detail. Sometimes satellite photos use false colours to represent the earth, because the camera is too far away to use natural light.

# 1. Sustainable Biomes
## Spatial Distribution

Biomes are the groupings of plant and animal communities that, combined, make up the Earth's total biosphere. It is a major terrestrial vegetation community, eg. a tropical forest.

The earth has four spheres; the **lithosphere**, **atmosphere**, **biosphere**, and **hydrosphere**.

The **lithosphere** is the 'rock' sphere, comprising of landforms and landscapes

The **hydrosphere** is the 'water' sphere, including water in all its form (liquid, solid, gas)

The **atmosphere** is the 'air' sphere, the layer of protective gases surrounding Earth

The **biosphere** is the 'life' sphere, including all life forms and all environments on or near Earth's surface that are capable of sustaining life.

## Identification of Biomes
### Forest and Woodland Biomes (General)

- **Dominant feature**: Trees; forests = tall, close-spaced, closed canopy; woodlands = smaller, spaced trees, open canopy.
- **Key influence**: Rainfall → higher rain = taller, denser trees (increased productivity).

---

### Tropical Forests

- **Location**: Near equator, between Tropics of Cancer & Capricorn.
- **Climate**: Very hot, high rainfall (>1500 mm/year).
- **Subtypes**:
    - **Tropical Rainforests**: Year-round rain, no dry season; trees >40 m, continuous canopy; highest productivity.
    - **Monsoon Forests**: Seasonal rain (monsoons); nearly continuous canopy.
- **Structure**:
    - **Canopy**: Interlocking branches (e.g., mahogany, teak, balsa); emergents rise above.
    - **Epiphytes/Lianas**: Grow/climb on trees for light.
    - **Understorey**: Saplings, palms, ferns (~15 m); low light.
    - **Forest floor**: Sparse plants, rapid decomposition → shallow humus.
- **Soils**: Infertile due to leaching despite high productivity.

---

### Temperate Forests

- **Rainfall**: High (>1500 mm/year).
- **Subtypes**:
    - **Warm Temperate**:
        - Location: Mid-latitudes (SE USA, S Japan, SE Australia, etc.).
        - Climate: Hot summers, mild winters, year-round moisture.
        - Vegetation: Broad-leafed **evergreens** (oaks, laurels, eucalypts).
    - **Cool Temperate**:
        - Location: Higher latitudes (NW Europe, NE USA, NE China).
        - Climate: Cold winters, high rainfall.
        - Vegetation: **Deciduous** trees (oak, maple, birch); shed leaves in winter.

---

### Taiga (Boreal/Coniferous Forest)

- **Location**: 50–60°N (Canada, Alaska, Siberia, Scandinavia).
- **Climate**: Long cold winters, short warm summers; 400–1000 mm precip (incl. snow); ~130-day growing season.
- **Vegetation**: Cold-tolerant **evergreen conifers** (pine, fir, spruce) with needle leaves.
- **Soils**: Thin, acidic, nutrient-poor.

---

### Woodlands

- **Rainfall**: Moderate, seasonal (700–1200 mm/year).
- **Role**: Transition between forests and grasslands/shrublands.
- **Subtypes**:
    - **Savanna Woodlands**:
        - Climate: Hot wet summers, warm dry winters.
        - Location: E Africa, Central America.
    - **Mediterranean Woodlands**:
        - Climate: Hot dry summers, cool wet winters.
        - Location: Mediterranean, S California, SW Australia, etc.
- **Adaptations**:
    - Evergreen trees (oaks, olives, eucalypts).
    - Waxy/leathery leaves, thick bark (fire/heat protection).
    - Wide roots or deep taproots for water.
- **Soils**: Laterised, hard gravelly upper layer.

---

### Grassland Biomes

- **General**: Moderately dry regions.

#### Savanna Grasslands

- **Location**: Africa (½ surface), Australia, S America, India.
- **Climate**: Hot wet summers, warm dry winters; 500–1000 mm/year.
- **Vegetation**: Grasses vary by rainfall/soil (e.g., Rhodes grass, red oat grass, star grass).
- **Soils**: Porous, rapid drainage, thin humus.

#### Temperate Grasslands

- **Location**: Pampas (Argentina), Steppes (Russia), Prairies (N America), Veldts (S Africa).
- **Climate**: Hot summers, cold winters, moderate seasonal rain.
- **Vegetation**: Taller grasses in wetter areas (prairies); shorter in drier (steppes); e.g., buffalo grass, purple needlegrass.
- **Soils**: Deep, dark, fertile; rich from grass root decay.

---

### Desert Biomes (Hot Deserts Focus)

- **Climate**: <250 mm/year; high daytime temps, low nights; high evaporation.
- **Soils**: Shallow, salty, infertile; poor humus; minerals rise upward.
- **Vegetation**: Sparse, stunted **xerophytes**:
    - **Drought evaders**: Short-lived (e.g., Sturt’s desert pea); seed dormancy.
    - **Drought escapers**: Wide/shallow or deep roots (e.g., ocotillo, ghost gum).
    - **Drought resisters**: Reduced leaves (e.g., cacti).
    - **Water conservers**: Succulents (store water in stems).

---

### Tundra

- **Location**: Arctic Circle to North Pole; south of taiga.
- **Climate**: Very cold (winter avg. –34°C, summer ~12°C); 150–250 mm precip; 50–60 day growing season.
- **Soil**: **Permafrost** (permanent frozen subsoil); slow formation; bogs/ponds on surface.
- **Vegetation**: Treeless; low shrubs, sedges, mosses, lichens, ~400 flower types; shallow roots; wind/cold-resistant; photosynthesis at low light/temp.
- **Biodiversity**: ~1700 plant species despite harsh conditions.

## Food Web
### Food Chains

- **Definition**: Linear sequence showing **energy flow** from one organism to another as each consumes the previous one (e.g., grass → rabbit → fox).

### Food Webs

- **Definition**: Complex network of **interconnected food chains** in an ecosystem, showing multiple feeding relationships and energy pathways.

### Producers

- **Definition**: Autotrophic organisms (usually plants, algae, or cyanobacteria) that **convert sunlight into energy** via photosynthesis; base of all food chains/webs.

### Primary Consumers

- **Definition**: **Herbivores** that eat producers directly (e.g., grasshoppers, rabbits, deer).

### Secondary Consumers

- **Definition**: **Carnivores or omnivores** that eat primary consumers (e.g., frogs, spiders, birds).

### Tertiary Consumers

- **Definition**: Top **carnivores** that eat secondary consumers; often **apex predators** (e.g., hawks, wolves, sharks).

### Herbivores

- **Definition**: Animals that **eat only plants/producers** (e.g., cows, caterpillars, elephants).

### Carnivores

- **Definition**: Animals that **eat only other animals** (e.g., lions, snakes, owls).

### Omnivores

- **Definition**: Animals that **eat both plants and animals** (e.g., humans, bears, pigs).

## Factors Influencing Biomes

### Climate Drivers

- **Latitude**:
    - Equator: Direct sunlight → hottest.
    - Poles: Slanted rays → coldest.
    - Earth’s tilt creates seasons: hemisphere toward sun = summer; away = winter.
- **Altitude**:
    - Temperature drops with height.
    - Ex: Mt Kilimanjaro (near equator) → base = warm rainforest; summit = cold tundra.
- **Mountains & Landforms**:
    - Block moist winds.
    - **Windward side**: Rain falls.
    - **Leeward side**: **Rain shadow** → dry → deserts form.
- **Ocean Currents**:
    - **Cold currents** near warm land → cool air → less rain → deserts (e.g., western Australia).
    - **Warm currents** → more evaporation → rain (e.g., eastern Australia).

---

### Soil in Biomes

- **Shaped by**: Temperature, rainfall, parent rock, vegetation.
- **Low latitudes (near equator)**:
    - Hot + wet → fast rock weathering + rapid plant decay → **deep, nutrient-rich soil**.
- **High latitudes (near poles)**:
    - Cold + dry → slow processes → **shallow, poor soil**.
- **Soil life**:
    - Bacteria, fungi, algae, earthworms, microbes.
    - Roles: decompose waste, improve aeration/drainage, add nutrients, mix soil layers.

---

### Climate + Soil → Biome

|Latitude|Temp|Rain|Soil|Typical Biome|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Low|High|High|Deep, rich|Tropical Rainforest|
|Mid|Moderate|Moderate|Fertile|Grasslands, Temperate Forests|
|High|Low|Low|Thin, poor|Tundra, Taiga|

_(Climate sets conditions; soil supports life → defines biome)_

## Biomes for Food, Materials & Fibres

### Biomass

- **Definition**: Total living matter (mostly plants) in an area.
- **Source**: Plants = **primary producers** via **photosynthesis**.
- **Measurement**: **Net Primary Productivity** (NPP) = energy from plant growth.
- **Climate link**:
- **High heat + water** → high NPP (e.g., **rainforests**).
- **Low heat/water** → low NPP (e.g., **deserts**).
- **Global fact**: **Oceans** = largest biomass producer (8× land; seaweed key).

---

### Human Use of Biomes

- **Purpose**: Food, fibres, materials, energy.
- **Two types**:
- **Commercial**: Grow/sell crops/livestock → profit.
- **Subsistence**: Grow just enough for family survival.

---

## Human Alterations to Biomes

### Overview

- Humans adapt/change biomes for settlement, food, resources.
- Early: Hunter-gatherers → low impact.
- Later: Population ↑ → **slash-and-burn**, large clearing → habitat loss.

### Main Impacts

- **Vegetation removal**
- **Land terracing**
- **Agriculture**
- **Irrigation**
- **Mining**
- **Urbanization** → **heat islands**, flooding.

---

### Vegetation Removal

- Common in farming (e.g., rice, sugarcane).
- Replaces natural biome with human-controlled one.
- Wildlife lost.

#### Dryland (Upland) Rice

- Clears forest for dry soil or flooded paddies.
- Relies on rain.
- **13% of rice area**, **4% production**.
- Used by ~100M in Asia.

#### Wet Rice (Terracing)

- Ancient method.
- Clears slope → builds **steps/terraces** + walls.
- Controls water run-off.
- **Reduces erosion** (some say), but:
    - Removes stabilizing plants → **landslides**.
    - Alters landscape/biome.

---

### Agriculture

- Dominant land use (e.g., **53% of Australia** = 410M ha).
- Ongoing clearing for crops/livestock.

---

### Irrigation

- Supplies water to crops/meat farming.
- **Diverts natural water** (rivers, groundwater).
- Dams/canals/pivots → disrupts flow.
- Changes biome water balance.

---

### Mining

- Extracts minerals (bauxite, gold, coal).
- **Open-pit mining** → major damage:
    - Air/water/soil pollution.
    - Toxic/radioactive leaks.
    - Habitat loss, erosion.
- **Water use**: High in extraction/processing → pollutes sources.
- **Long-term impact**: Damage lasts post-closure.
## Environmental Impacts of Human Alterations

### Habitat & Biodiversity Loss

- **Main cause**: Habitat destruction (agriculture, urban, forestry, water projects).
- **Result**: Species **endangered/extinct**; ecosystems fragmented.
- **Ex: New Zealand**
    - Pre-human: **85% forest**.
    - Now: **23% forest**.
    - **Maori**: Fires → cleared 1/3 by 1600.
    - **Europeans**: Half remaining forest → farms/towns.
    - Introduced species (e.g., **stoats**) → kill natives (e.g., **kiwi**: only 10% chicks survive).
    - Wetlands/rivers → dams, irrigation, pollution.

---

### Drainage

- **Purpose**: Remove excess water → prevent waterlogging, enable farming/building.
- **Impact**:
    - Destroys **wetland biomes**.
    - Reduces water for surrounding plants/animals.
- **Ex: Macquarie Marshes (NSW)**
    - Major waterbird site.
    - Now **40–50% smaller** due to drainage + irrigation.
    - **20% of Australia’s birds** rely on wetlands.
    - Migratory birds affected globally.

---

### Water Pollution

- **Causes**: Waste dumping, runoff (industrial, farm, urban).
- **Effects**:
    - Overloads natural cleaning → downstream contamination.
    - Harms fish, birds, wildlife.
- **Ex: San Francisco Bay Delta (USA)**
    - NRDC fights excess water withdrawal → protects endangered fish.

## Salinity from Human Alterations

### Causes

- **Land use change**: Clearing native deep-rooted plants → ↑ groundwater rise.
- **Irrigation**: Shallow-rooted crops (e.g., cotton, wheat) + watering → water infiltrates → **watertable rises** → **salt mobilised** to surface.
- **Vegetation loss**: Drought, fire, overgrazing, clearing → **soil erosion** → exposes **sodic/saline subsoils** → crusting, poor structure.
- **Urban/industrial waste**: High salt input.
- **Soil compaction** (roads): ↓ permeability → ponding → evaporation → **salt accumulation**.

### Process (Irrigation Example)

1. Native plants removed.
2. Shallow crops planted.
3. Excess water infiltrates.
4. Watertable rises → brings salt up.
5. Water evaporates → **salt left on surface**.

### Impacts

- **Freshwater rivers**: ↑ salt → harms plants/animals → **biodiversity loss**.
- **Ecosystems**: Entire systems damaged.
- **Economic/environmental cost**: High, especially with warming + population pressure.

## Environmental Factors → Agricultural Yields

### Two Categories

- **Biotic** (living): Animals, insects, birds, bees.
    - **Harm**: Goats/insects eat crops.
    - **Help**: Birds spread seeds/manure; bees **pollinate**.
- **Abiotic** (non-living): Temp, rain, water, soil, light, landforms.
    - **Key process**: **Photosynthesis** (needs light, water, O₂, minerals).

> **Fact**: 3 crops (rice, maize, wheat) = **60% world food energy**.

---

### Temperature

- Crops have **ideal temps**.
- **Warm/humid**: Some thrive.
- **Cool/mild**: Others (e.g., **broccoli** → Lockyer Valley, QLD).
- **Warmer climates** → need **more water** (↑ evaporation).
- **Humid** → less water needed.

---

### Australia’s Climate & Agriculture

- **Rainfall** = #1 factor.
- **Patterns**:
    - **Interior**: Low rain + high evaporation → only **extensive grazing**.
    - **North (e.g., Kimberley)**: Summer rain → high evaporation → 1 cattle/25–30 ha.
    - **South (NSW, VIC, TAS, SA, SW WA)**: Winter rain → lower evaporation → **cereal crops**, sheep belt.
- **Past issues**: Overuse → **erosion, degradation**.
- **Controls**: e.g., **Goyder Line (1865)** → limits wheat in dry north SA.

## Impact of Water Scarcity & Pollution on Food Production

### Water Use in Australia

- **Agriculture**: **70%** of total water use.
- **Land clearing**: **100M ha** forest/woodland → farming.

---

### Water Scarcity

- **Effect**: No irrigation → ↓ crop yields → ↑ prices.
- **Australia**: Dry climate → ongoing issue.
- **Global (NENA region)**:
    - **2050**: Fresh water ↓ **50%**.
    - Ag uses **>85%** water → demand rising.
- **Quote (FAO)**: Ag = largest user but key to survival ($95B value).

---

### Water Pollution & Quality Decline

|Cause|Impact|
|---|---|
|**Intensive farming (Murray River)**|↑ Salinity → undrinkable, corrodes pipes (Adelaide)|
|**Erosion**|↑ Sediment → cloudy rivers|
|**Fertiliser runoff (N, P)**|**Algal blooms** → toxins, fish deaths, low O₂, bad smell/taste|

> **Turbidity**: Cloudiness from suspended particles.

---

### Air Pollution

- **Effects on crops**:
    - ↓ Yield
    - Premature death
    - Stunted growth
    - Leaf damage
- **Types**:
    - **Widespread (oxidants)**: Cover 100sq km².
    - **Local (sulfur oxides)**: From coal/petroleum → poison in defined areas.

## Impact of Land Degradation & Competing Land Uses on Food Production

### Overview

- **Ag pressure** → **erosion, salinity, biodiversity loss** = **environmental degradation**.
- **2/3 of ag land degraded**; some irreversible.
- **Cycle**: Poor land → ↓ yields → ↑ costs.

---

### Erosion

- **Natural** but **accelerated** by grazing, clearing.
- **Past**: No rotation → soil exhaustion.

---

### Soil Acidity

- **Cause**: N-fertilisers, clover/lucerne.
- **Effects**:
    - ↓ Nutrient availability (Ca, Mg, etc.).
    - ↑ Toxicity (Al, Fe, Mn).
    - ↓ Crop/pasture growth.
- **Fix**: Gypsum (alkali); **GRDC Soil Constraints Initiative** (2015).

---

### Soil Salinity

- **Scale**:
    - Now: **2.5M ha** affected.
    - 2050–2100: **12M ha** (~4.5% cultivated).
- **Types**:
    - **Dryland**: Clearing deep-rooted natives → ↑ watertable → salt rises.
    - **Irrigated**: Overwatering → same.
- **Signs**: Dead trees, bare patches, salt weeds, saline streams.
- **Impact**: Kills plants, ↓ yields, undrinkable water.

---

### Competing Land Uses

- **Ag vs. urban**: Population clusters (coastal) → pressure on prime land.
- **Result**: Loss of farmland → ↑ food prices, ↓ production.

### Competing Land Uses vs. Food Production

|Use|Conflict|Impact on Food|
|---|---|---|
|**Mining**|**Priority** in QLD (Bowen & Surat Basins): coal/gas vs. wheat, barley, livestock|High-value minerals → ag land lost; drought-resilient farmland at risk|
|**Recreation**|Golf courses, resorts → need water + scenic land|Takes prime ag areas (good soil, water)|
|**Urban Expansion**|Coastal sprawl → most fertile, high-rain areas|↓ Arable land; ↑ pollution, erosion|
|**Biofuel**|Crops (corn, sugarcane) for **fuel** vs. **food**|↑ Demand → less food, higher prices; competes with soap industry (tallow)|

**Trend**: Non-food uses win due to **economic value** or **liveability**.

## Patterns of Settlement

### Historical Role

- Cities once **protected** people (e.g., **Dubrovnik’s walls**, 12th–17th c. → deter invaders).

### Modern Urban Settlements

- **Definition**: Areas where most people **don’t rely on agriculture**.
- **Size threshold**: Varies (2,500–20,000 pop. by country).
- **Activities**: Work, education, culture, services.

---

### Settlement Hierarchy (Smallest → Largest)

| Type            | Description           | Key Features                                         |
| --------------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hamlet**      | Tiny rural settlement | Few houses, minimal services                         |
| **Village**     | Small rural cluster   | Homes + basic commercial, smaller than town          |
| **Town**        | Local centre          | Name, gov, services (doctors, schools, banks)        |
| **City**        | Major hub             | Commerce, culture, significant pop. & importance     |
| **Metropolis**  | Capital/region centre | High pop., universities, hospitals, **urban sprawl** |
| **Megacity**    | Giant city            | **>10M pop.**                                        |
| **Conurbation** | Merged urban area     | Multiple cities + suburbs fused                      |

## Urbanisation: Growth & Impacts

### **Growth**

- **Now**: 3.2B urban → **2030**: **5B** (3/5 people).
