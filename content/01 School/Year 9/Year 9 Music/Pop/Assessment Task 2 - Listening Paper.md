# Concepts of Music

## Duration

**Duration** refers to the arrangements of sound in relation to time.

If you can tap your foot or nod your head to the music, it is because the time has been divided into **beats**.

If you cannot hear or feel a **beat**, the music is said to be in **free time**.

When pointing out the **speed** of the beat you are referring to its **tempo**.

**Tempo** changes affect the music's **mood** - **speeding** up can make the music feel exciting and **slowing** down makes it more relaxed. If it says the same, it is said to be steady.

When beats accents are in regular patterns of:

- **Two** - it is in duple metre, often with two-four time signature
- **Three** - it is in triple metre, often with three-four time signature
- **Four** - it is in quadruple metre, often with four-four time signature

**Metre** is therefore the pattern of the beats' accents.

The **rhythm** (organisation of sounds and silences) may be made up of mainly **long** or **short** notes, or a combination. **Short** notes make the rhythm feel **busy** and **long** notes make the rhythm feel **calm**.

When a **rhythm** is repeated a number of times (making a predictable pattern), it is called a rhythmic **ostinato**.

## Pitch

**Pitch** deals with sounds in terms of how **relatively** **high** and **low** they are.

Instruments or sound sources that you can play 'Happy Birthday' on have a **definite** pitch.

Instruments or sound sources that you can't play 'Happy Birthday' on have an **indefinite** pitch.

A **melody** is the part of music that you usually sing or whistle - it is the **tune**.

The **pitch** placement of the melody refers to how **high** or how **low** the **melody** is.

**Contour** refers to the **shapes** that the notes of a melody make. A contour may be:

- **Flat**
- **Ascending**
- **Descending**
- **Wavy**
- **Jagged**

**Articulation** is about the different **ways** that notes of a **melody** can be played. For example:

- **Legato**: smooth and flowing
- **Staccato**: short, crisp and jumpy

Like sentences, melodies can be divided into **phrases**. A melodic phrase refers to the notes between breaths if the melody was to be sung. A long phrase feels like it comes close to 'running out of breath'.

**Harmony** occurs when two or more notes are sounded together (**chords**).

**Harmony** can sound:

- **Consonant**: sounds pleasant and harmonious
- **Dissonant**: sounds relatively jarring and harsh

## Structure

**Structure** deals with the design or plan of a composition.

Most music is made up of distinct **sections**.

These sections are sometimes labelled by their **function** - for example: **introduction, verse, bridge, chorus and coda.**

Sometimes sections are labelled with a letter. For example the first section can be labelled as the 'A' section and this of course would be followed by the 'B' section and so on.

An **introduction** often establishes various aspects of the music, such as its **metre, tempo, instrumentation and style**.

A **coda** is the concluding section of a piece and has music which sounds like it is about to finish.

**Binary** form has two distinct sections which can be represented as AB.

**Ternary** form has three sections, the 3rd being a repetition of the first, ABA.

**Rondo** form has many sections, alternating with a return to the first such as ABACA.

Writing music that is appealing enough to keep listeners interest requires a delicate **balance** between unity and contrast,

**Unity** is created by repeating music ideas.

**Contrast** is created by introducing new musical ideas.

## Tone Colour

**Tone colour** refers to the instruments and sound sources used in the music.

If you can't name an instrument, list its **sound source**. Sound sources include: **wood, metal, string, skin, electronic and vocal.**

**Sound production** tells you how the instrument is played. Methods include: **hitting, blowing, shaking, scraping and plucking.**

**Timbre** refers to the sound quality of those instruments, for example: **bright, dark, thin, brittle, buzzing, breathy, harsh, swishy, luscious and percussive.**

Instruments can change their timbre for example by:

- Using a **bow** instead of **plucking**
- Using **brushes** instead of **sticks**
- Using a **mute**
- Using different **expressive** **techniques** (like **vibrato** or **flutter tonguing**)
- Using **electronic** effects, like a **distortion** pedal

Different types of **ensembles** (group of instruments) are chosen to perform a certain style, for example: **orchestra, rock or pop band, choir, steel drum band, world music band, jazz band etc.**

## Texture

**Texture** refers to the **layers** of sound and how they **relate** to each other.

**Layers** can be identified by referring to the **instruments** used. Here is an example of how a rock song might be seen in terms of layers of sound:

- **Vocal** layer (singing a **melody**)
- **Guitar** layer (playing **chords**)
- **Bass** layer (playing the **chord's main note**)
- **Drum** layer (playing **rhythms**)

The **role** of each layer describes its **musical contribution**. For example, a layer providing:

- **melodic** ideas - have a **melodic** role
- **chords** or **bass** notes related to chords - have a **harmonic** role
- **rhythms** - have a **rhythmic** role
- **sound effects** - have an **atmospheric** role

**Density** refers to the **thickness**/**thinness** of the music. If there are lots of layers playing at once, the music sounds thicker than if there are only a few.

When layers enter one at a time, the density gradually thickens. The term applied to this is **staggered entry**.

Composers can make the music more interesting by **varying** the **combinations** of layers.

## Dynamics and Expressive Techniques

**Dynamics and Expressive Techniques** examines the **mood** of the music and how it is **created**.

There are many **moods** that can be **conveyed** in music. Composers manipulate all of the concepts of music in order to express these different moods. Music can be: **calm, peaceful, exciting, thrilling, lazy sluggish, relaxed, laid back, energetic, lively, weak, fragile, powerful, strong, aggressive, grand, stately, clumsy and peaceful.**

Dynamics refers to the levels of volume at which music is played. **eg. very soft, moderately soft, soft, moderate, moderately loud, loud, very loud.**

Dynamic changes can be **sudden** or **gradual**.

Composers use **dynamics** to contribute to the **mood** of the music. For example, anger is often expressed at a **loud** volume whereas calm is expressed at **gentler** volume.

**Expressive techniques** add **ornaments** to the mood of the music. For example, **vibrato** can help to convey sadness or sweetness on long notes, **bends** can help a blues line sound mournful and **fast hammer-ons** and **take-offs** can add to the excitement of the music.

Expressive techniques include: **vibrato** (Wobbling the pitch), **pitch bends**, **glissandi** (slides), **flutter-tonguing**, **trills** (rapid alternation of two notes), **hammer-ons**, **take-offs**, **harmonics**, **electronic effects** etc.
