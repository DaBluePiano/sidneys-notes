# 1. Introduction to Waves

- **Definition**: A wave is a disturbance that travels through a substance, transferring energy from one place to another without transferring matter.
- **Types of Waves**:
    - **Mechanical Waves**: Require a medium (e.g., sound, water waves).
    - **Electromagnetic Waves**: Do not require a medium (e.g., light).
- **Transverse vs Longitudinal**:

| Type         | Description                                     | Particle Motion                            | Examples             |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------- |
| Transverse   | Wave direction perpendicular to particle motion | Up and down (perpendicular to wave)        | Light, water ripples |
| Longitudinal | Wave direction parallel to particle motion      | Back and forth (compressions/rarefactions) | Sound                |

# 2. Wave Properties

- **Anatomy of a Transverse Wave**:
    - **Crest**: Highest point of the wave.
    - **Trough**: Lowest point of the wave.
    - **Wavelength (λ)**: Distance between two adjacent crests or troughs (in meters).
    - **Amplitude**: Maximum displacement from the midline to crest or trough (measures energy).
    - **Frequency (f)**: Number of waves passing a point per second (in Hertz, Hz).
    - **Period (T)**: Time for one complete wave (T = 1/f).
- **Wave Speed**: How fast the wave travels through a medium (in m/s).
- **Wave Equation**:

$v=f \times \lambda v$

Where:

- $v$ = speed (m/s)
- $f$ = frequency (Hz)
- $λ$ = wavelength (m)

# 3. Electromagnetic Waves

- **Characteristics**: Transverse waves that travel at the speed of light (3 × 10^8 m/s in vacuum); no medium required; consist of oscillating electric and magnetic fields.
- **Electromagnetic Spectrum**: Waves arranged by increasing frequency (decreasing wavelength); all have same speed in vacuum but different energies/uses.

| Type          | Wavelength Range (m)           | Frequency Range (Hz)             | Energy    | Uses                             | Dangers                           |
| ------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------------- | --------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Radio         | $> 10^{-1}$                    | $< 3 × 10^9$                     | Low       | Broadcasting, communication      | Minimal                           |
| Microwave     | $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-1}$         | $3 × 10^9$ to $3 × 10^{11}$      | Low       | Cooking, radar, phones           | Tissue heating                    |
| Infrared      | $7 × 10^{-7}$ to $10^{-3}$     | $3 × 10^{11}$ to 4 × $10^{14}$   | Medium    | Remote controls, thermal imaging | Burns                             |
| Visible Light | $4 × 10^{-7}$ to 7 × $10^{-7}$ | $4 × 10^{14}$ to $7.5 × 10^{14}$ | Medium    | Vision, photosynthesis           | Eye damage (intense light)        |
| Ultraviolet   | $10^{-8}$ to $4 × 10^{-7}$     | $7.5 × 10^{14}$ to $3 × 10^{16}$ | High      | Sterilization, vitamin D         | Sunburn, skin cancer              |
| X-ray         | $10^{-11}$ to $10^{-8}$        | $3 × 10^{16}$ to $3 × 10^{19}$   | High      | Medical imaging                  | Cell damage, cancer               |
| Gamma         | $< 10^{-11}$                   | $> 3 × 10^{19}$                  | Very High | Cancer treatment, sterilization  | Radiation sickness, mutations<br> |

# 4. Light Waves
- Light is part of the EM spectrum (visible range).
- **Behavior**:
    - **Reflection**: Bounces off surfaces; follows Law of Reflection (angle of incidence = angle of reflection).
    - **Absorption**: Energy absorbed by material (e.g., colors: objects appear colored based on reflected wavelengths).
    - **Refraction**: Bends when passing between media due to speed change.
    - **Transmission**: Passes through (transparent: clear; translucent: scattered; opaque: blocked).
- Colors: Objects reflect specific wavelengths; others absorbed.

# 5. Sound Waves

- **Definition**: Longitudinal mechanical waves produced by vibrations; require a medium.
- **Production**: Vibrations create alternating compressions (high pressure) and rarefactions (low pressure).
- **Properties**:
    - Speed: ~340 m/s in air; faster in denser media (e.g., solids > liquids > gases).
    - Pitch: Determined by frequency (high f = high pitch).
    - Loudness: Determined by amplitude/energy (measured in decibels, dB).
- Cannot travel in vacuum.

![Physics Tutorial: Sound Waves as Pressure Waves](https://www.physicsclassroom.com/Class/sound/u11l1c2.gif)

- **Comparison to Light Waves**:

| Aspect     | Light Waves                  | Sound Waves                 |
| ---------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Type       | Transverse, Electromagnetic  | Longitudinal, Mechanical    |
| Medium     | Can travel in vacuum         | Requires medium (no vacuum) |
| Speed      | $3 × 10^8$ m/s               | about $340$ m/s in air      |
| Production | Electromagnetic oscillations | Vibrations                  |
