# Rocky Road Exam Notes

## 1. Earth's Structure

- **Layers of the Earth**: The Earth is composed of several layers, each with distinct properties, composition, and thickness.
- The structure contributes to plate tectonics through convection currents in the mantle.

| Layer         | Description                                                                                                     | Thickness       | Composition                                         |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Crust         | Hard outer layer; thinnest layer. Divided into continental (thicker, less dense) and oceanic (thinner, denser). | 5-100 km        | Rocks (granite for continental, basalt for oceanic) |
| Lithosphere   | Rigid outer zone including crust and upper mantle; broken into tectonic plates.                                 | ~100 km         | Solid rock                                          |
| Asthenosphere | Semi-molten zone beneath lithosphere; allows plates to move due to plastic flow.                                | 100-200 km      | Partially molten rock                               |
| Mantle        | Thickest layer; solid but flows slowly over time due to heat and pressure.                                      | ~2900 km        | Peridotite rock                                     |
| Outer Core    | Liquid layer; generates Earth's magnetic field.                                                                 | ~2200 km        | Molten iron and nickel                              |
| Inner Core    | Solid due to immense pressure; hottest layer.                                                                   | ~1220 km radius | Solid iron and nickel                               |
![Explainer: Earth — layer by layer|480x337](https://www.sciencenewsforstudents.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/1030_Earthlayers.png)

## 2. Plate Tectonics

- **Theory**: Earth's lithosphere is divided into tectonic plates that move due to convection currents in the mantle (hot material rises, cools, sinks).
- **Plate Boundaries**: Where plates interact, leading to geological activity like earthquakes and volcanoes.

| Boundary Type | Description                                         | Motion               | Features/Effects                            | Examples                                                 |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Convergent    | Plates collide; denser plate subducts.              | Towards each other   | Trenches, mountains, volcanoes, earthquakes | Andes (ocean-continent), Himalayas (continent-continent) |
| Divergent     | Plates separate; new crust forms from rising magma. | Away from each other | Mid-ocean ridges, rift valleys              | Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift                    |
| Transform     | Plates slide past each other horizontally.          | Side by side         | Faults, earthquakes (no new crust)          | San Andreas Fault<br>                                    |

## 3. Continental Drift

- **Theory**: Proposed by Alfred Wegener; continents were once joined in supercontinent Pangaea ~200-300 million years ago and drifted apart.
- **Evidence**:
    - Continents fit like a jigsaw (e.g., South America and Africa).
    - Identical fossils/plants on separated continents (e.g., Mesosaurus, Glossopteris).
    - Matching rock formations and mountain ranges across oceans.
    - Past glacial evidence in current tropical areas.
    - Paleoclimatic evidence (e.g., coal in Antarctica).

## 4. Sea Floor Spreading

- **Theory**: Proposed by Harry Hess; new oceanic crust forms at mid-ocean ridges from rising magma, spreads outward, and old crust subducts at trenches.
- **Process**: Mantle convection causes divergence at ridges; crust ages away from ridge.
- **Evidence**:
    - Age of sea floor rocks (youngest at ridges, oldest at trenches).
    - Magnetic striping (reversals recorded in rock).
    - Rift valleys and underwater mountains at ridges.
    - Sediment thicker near continents.

## 5. Volcanoes

- **Definition**: Opening in Earth's crust where magma erupts as lava; formed at plate boundaries or hot spots.
- **Formation**: Magma rises due to pressure from gases; erupts through vent.
- **Types/Features**: Stratovolcano (steep, explosive), Shield (gentle, fluid lava).
- **Locations**: Mostly at convergent (subduction zones) and divergent boundaries; Ring of Fire (Pacific).
- **Eruption Products**: Lava, ash, gases (CO₂, SO₂, H₂S), pyroclastic flows.

| Feature       | Description                               |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Magma Chamber | Reservoir of molten rock beneath volcano. |
| Conduit/Pipe  | Channel for magma to rise.                |
| Vent          | Opening where lava erupts.                |
| Crater        | Depression at summit.                     |
| Lava Flow     | Molten rock on surface.                   |
| Ash Cloud     | Plume of volcanic ash and gases.          |

- **Practical**: Model volcano using baking soda, vinegar to simulate eruption.

## 6. Earthquakes

- **Cause**: Sudden release of strain energy when locked plates snap; occurs at plate boundaries.
- **Focus (Hypocenter)**: Point underground where rupture starts.
- **Epicentre**: Point on surface directly above focus; greatest damage.
- **Distribution**: Along plate boundaries (e.g., Ring of Fire).

## 7. Tsunamis

- **Cause**: Undersea earthquakes displace water, generating large waves.
- **Effects**: Devastating coastal flooding.

## 8. Seismic Waves

- **Body Waves** (travel through interior): P (fastest, compressional), S (slower, shear).
- **Surface Waves** (travel along surface, most damaging): Love (horizontal), Rayleigh (rolling).

|Wave Type|Motion|Speed|Travels Through|Effects|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|P (Primary)|Compressional (push-pull)|Fastest (~6 km/s)|Solids, liquids, gases|Least damaging|
|S (Secondary)|Shear (side-to-side)|Slower (~3.5 km/s)|Solids only|More damaging|
|Love|Horizontal shear|Slow|Surface|Building sway|
|Rayleigh|Rolling (up-down, back-forth)|Slowest|Surface|Ground rolling, severe damage<br><br>![Seismic waves — Science Learning Hub](https://www.sciencelearn.org.nz/_next/image?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.datocms-assets.com%2F117510%2F1722316285-01_03_01_05_dg1_eqsfourearthwaves_update.jpg%3Fw%3D1840%26h%3D2605.0077279752704&w=1920&q=85&dpl=dpl_4qyw5SfmENFS6kX3PoMrXUkF3Nc1)<br><br>[sciencelearn.org.nz](https://www.sciencelearn.org.nz/resources/340-seismic-waves)<br><br>Diagram of types of seismic waves (P, S, Love, Rayleigh).|

- **Seismometer**: Instrument to detect waves; records seismograph.
- **Locating Epicentre**: Use time difference between P and S waves from multiple stations; triangulate.

## 9. Measuring Earthquakes

- **Richter Scale**: Measures magnitude (energy released); logarithmic (each unit = 10x amplitude, 30x energy).
- **Mercalli Scale**: Measures intensity (observed effects); I (not felt) to XII (total destruction).

|Richter Magnitude|Description|Mercalli Intensity|Effects|
|---|---|---|---|
|<3.5|Micro|I-II|Not felt or barely noticeable|
|3.5-5.4|Minor/Light|III-V|Felt indoors, hanging objects swing|
|5.5-6.0|Moderate|VI-VII|Damage to weak structures|
|6.1-6.9|Strong|VIII|Considerable damage, buildings shift|
|7.0-7.9|Major|IX-X|Serious damage, structures collapse|
|8.0+|Great|XI-XII|Total destruction<br><br>![Relationship Between Richter Magnitude and Modified Mercalli Intensity \|  Missouri Department of Natural Resources](https://dnr.mo.gov/sites/dnr/files/media/image/2020/05/earthquake-mercalli-richter.gif)<br><br>[dnr.mo.gov](https://dnr.mo.gov/land-geology/hazards/earthquakes/science/relationship-between-richter-magnitude-modified-mercalli-intensity)<br><br>Comparison of Richter and Mercalli scales.|

## 10. Faults and Folds

- **Folds**: Bending of rock layers due to compression.
    - Anticline: Upward arch (A shape).
    - Syncline: Downward trough (U shape).
- **Faults**: Breaks where rocks move.
    - Normal: Tension, hanging wall down.
    - Reverse: Compression, hanging wall up.
    - Strike-Slip: Shear, horizontal movement (left or right lateral).

|Structure Type|Description|Associated Force|
|---|---|---|
|Anticline|Upward fold|Compression|
|Syncline|Downward fold|Compression|
|Normal Fault|Hanging wall drops|Tension|
|Reverse Fault|Hanging wall rises|Compression|

## 11. Practical Investigations

- Model volcano: Simulate eruption with baking soda + vinegar.
- Model seismometer: Build to detect P and S waves.
- Locate epicentre: Use seismograph data and triangulation.

**Tips for Exam**:

- Recall key terms from glossary (e.g., lithosphere, subduction, seismic waves).
- Practice labeling diagrams of Earth's layers, plate boundaries, volcanoes, earthquakes.
- Understand evidence for theories (continental drift, sea floor spreading).
- Differentiate scales (Richter: magnitude/energy; Mercalli: intensity/effects).
- Apply concepts to real-world examples (e.g., Ring of Fire for volcanoes/earthquakes).
