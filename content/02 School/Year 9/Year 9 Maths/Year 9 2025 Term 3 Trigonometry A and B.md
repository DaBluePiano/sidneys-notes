# Trigonometry A

## Trigonometric Ratios

![[image-48.png|500x430]]

BC is opposite the angle $θ$, so we can call it the **opposite side**.

AB is next to the angle $θ$, so we can call it the **adjacent side**.

AC is opposite the right angle, so we can call it the **hypotenuse**.

There are three trigonometric ratios in Trigonometry:

$$\sin \theta = \frac {O}{H}$$

$$\cos \theta = \frac {A}{H}$$

$$\tan \theta = \frac {O}{A}$$

Where $H$ is the hypotenuse, $O$ is the opposite side and $A$ is the adjacent side. Note that we can also abbreviate sine, cosine and tangent to $\sin$, $\cos$ and $\tan$.

You can use SOH CAH TOA to memorise these three trigonometric ratios.

## Finding a Side

Steps to find a missing side in a right-angled triangle:

1. Identify the reference angle.
2. Label the side lengths as hypotenuse, opposite, and adjacent.
3. Choose the ratio which has one of the required sides given and the missing side:
    - **Sine**: opposite and hypotenuse
    - **Cosine**: adjacent and hypotenuse
    - **Tangent**: opposite and adjacent
4. Substitute the information you know into the chosen trigonometric ratio.
5. Solve for the unknown.

## Finding an Angle

To find a missing angle in a right-angled triangle, we can calculate the **inverse** of the trigonometric ratio.

The inverse effectively undoes the trigonometric ratio. If $\sin \theta = x$, then we undo the sine by using the inverse operation $\sin^{-1}$.

$$\theta = \sin^{-1} x$$

When evaluating inverse trigonometric functions, make sure that your calculator is in degrees mode.

While we may represent the inverse trigonometric functions using an index of $-1$, they are not reciprocals of the original functions, $\sin^{-1}\theta \neq \frac{1}{\sin\theta}$.

To find missing angles in right-angled triangles, we require at least two known sides in order to use trigonometric ratios.

To determine which trigonometric ratio we can use to find the unknown angle, we must label each of the two known side lengths of the triangle as either opposite, adjacent or the hypotenuse.

We then solve for the angle by applying the inverse of that ratio.

1. Identify the reference angle.
2. Label the side lengths as hypotenuse, opposite, and adjacent.
3. Choose the ratio which has two of the required sides given in relation to the angle:
    - **Sine**: opposite and hypotenuse
    - **Cosine**: adjacent and hypotenuse
    - **Tangent**: opposite and adjacent
4. Substitute the information you know into the chosen trigonometric ratio.
5. Solve for the unknown.

## Applications of Trigonometry

To find unknown sides in right-angled triangles, we require at least one angle (other than the right angle) and one other side length.

To find missing angles in right-angled triangles, we require at least two known sides and we use the inverse trigonometric ratios.

To solve trigonometry problems, in a real-world scenario, we can follow these steps:

1. Draw the diagram as a right-angled triangle and label the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent.
2. Label the given side lengths and angle measures.
3. Choose the trigonometric ratio that applies to the given information.
4. Substitute the known values into the trigonometric ratio and solve for the unknown.
5. Give the answer in the context of the situation.

# Trigonometry B

## Angles of Elevation and Depression

An **angle of elevation** is the angle from the lower object to the higher one.

An **angle of depression** is the angle from the higher object to the lower one.

You can imagine this as:

Angle of elevation is the angle of looking up to an object, and angle of depression is the angle of looking down to an object.

The angle of elevation is always equal to the angle of depression because they are alternate angles in parallel lines.

When given the angle of elevation or depression between two objects, we will always be able to model their relative position using a right-angled triangle.

## Compass and True Bearings

### Compass Bearings

![[image-60.png|217x208]]

The four main directions of a compass are known as cardinal directions. They are north (N), east (E), south (S)(S), and west (W).

In between each of these cardinal directions are intermediate directions of northeast (NE), southeast (SE), southwest (SW), and northwest (NW) that are 45° from the two cardinal directions closest.

There are smaller divisions shown on the compass as well.

A **compass bearing** describes the location of a point using:

- The starting direction of either north or south
- The acute angle needed to rotate
- The direction to rotate, east or west.

### True Bearings

A **three-figure bearing** is:

- Measured from north (N)
- Measured in a clockwise direction
- Written using three figures

T is often, but not always, used to describe a true bearing. If the angle measure is less than 100° it would be written as 040° or 040°T.
