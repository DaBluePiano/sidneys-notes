## Core Ideas & Techniques

### Pronumerals & Algebraic Manipulation

- Treat variables like numbers for addition, subtraction, multiplication; **never divide** by an expression that could be zero.
- Combine **like terms** (same pronumeral powers), keep terms arranged by degree for clarity.

### Index Laws (for Nonzero $a$, Integers $m,n$)

$$
a^m a^n = a^{m+n},\qquad \frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n},\qquad (a^m)^n=a^{mn},\qquad a^0=1.
$$

### Expanding & Patterns

- Distribute: $(a+b)c=ac+bc$.
- Useful expansions:
$$
(x+y)^2=x^2+2xy+y^2,\quad (x-y)^2=x^2-2xy+y^2,\quad (x+y)(x-y)=x^2-y^2.
$$

### Factoring Strategies

1. Factor out the greatest common factor (GCF).
2. Recognise special forms: difference of squares, perfect squares.
3. For quadratics $x^2+bx+c$, find $p,q$ with $p+q=b,\ pq=c$ so $x^2+bx+c=(x+p)(x+q)$.

### Algebraic Fractions

- **Simplify:** factor numerator and denominator then cancel common factors.
- **Operations:** add/subtract using common denominator; multiply numerators & denominators; divide by reciprocal.
- Always state excluded values where denominator $=0$.

### Solving Linear Equations

- Expand, collect like terms, isolate variable: from $ax+b=c$ get $x=\dfrac{c-b}{a}$ (when $a\ne0$).
- Clear fractions by multiplying through by the least common denominator (LCD), track excluded values.

### Quadratics — Solution Methods

- **Factorisation** when possible.
- **Quadratic formula** (always works):
$$
x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a},\qquad \Delta=b^2-4ac.
$$
- Interpret $\Delta$: $\Delta>0$ two real roots, $\Delta=0$ one repeated root, $\Delta<0$ complex roots.
- **Completing the square** converts $ax^2+bx+c$ to vertex form $a(x-h)^2+k$ where $h=-\dfrac{b}{2a}$.

### Simultaneous Equations

- **Linear–linear:** use elimination or substitution.
- **Linear–nonlinear:** substitute linear expression into nonlinear to reduce to one variable; check solutions.

---

## Quick Checklist when Solving Problems

1. Look for GCF and factoring opportunities.
2. Track domain restrictions (denominators, even roots).
3. Simplify step-by-step; check final answers in original equation.
