# Expanding Brackets

Expanding brackets is routine in arithmetic. For example, we can calculate $7 \times 61$ as

$$7 \times (60 + 1) = 7 \times 60 + 7 \times 1$$

which quickly gives the result $7 \times 61 = 420 + 7 = 427$. The algebraic version of this procedure can be written as:

> [!NOTE] Expanding Brackets in Algebra
> $$a(x+y)=ax+ay\quad\mathrm{and}\quad(x+y)a=xa+ya$$

There may then be like terms to collect.

## Expanding the Product of Two Bracketed terms

Expand one pair of brackets, then expand the other pair of brackets. Then collect any like terms.

## Special Expansions

These three identities are important and must be memorised. Examples of these expansions occur constantly, and knowing the formulae greatly simplifies the working.

> [!NOTE] Special Expansions
> - Square of a sum:  $(A + B)^2 = A^2 + 2AB + B^2$
> - Square of a difference: $(A - B)^2 = A^2 - 2AB + B^2$
> - Difference of squares: $(A + B)(A - B) = A^2 - B^2$
