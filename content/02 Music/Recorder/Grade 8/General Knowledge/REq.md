# Part 1: Questions Based Directly on the Score

## (i) Notation, Terminology, and Fundamentals

- **Identification:**
    - Name this note: `[Insert specific note from score]`
    - Name this rest: `[Insert specific rest from score]`
    - What is the meaning of this dynamic marking: `[e.g., pp, mf, sfz]`?
    - Identify this articulation mark: `[e.g., staccato, tenuto, accent]`.
    - What does this Italian term (`[e.g., Allegro, dolce, cresc.]`) instruct the performer to do?
    - What is the key signature of this piece? What scale does it represent?
    - What is the time signature? What does it tell us about the meter?
    - What is the title of the piece? Does it have a specific meaning or programmatic element?
- **Explanation:**
    - Explain the effect of the `[specific articulation/sign]` in bar `[X]` on the musical phrase.
    - How does the composer use dynamics in section `[Y]` to create contrast?
    - What is the function of the `[e.g., pedal mark, octave sign, trill]` written here?

## (ii) Tonality and Harmony

- **Identification:**
    - What is the primary key/tonality of the opening section?
    - Identify the bar number and key of the first key change (modulation).
    - At bar `[X]`, the music modulates. To what key does it modulate?
    - Is this piece tonal, modal, pentatonic, whole-tone, or atonal? Justify your answer with reference to the score.
- **Explanation:**
    - How does the composer pivot from the tonic key to the relative major/minor?
    - Describe the harmonic progression in the cadence at bar `[Y]` (e.g., perfect cadence, interrupted cadence).
    - If the piece uses a non-tonal system (e.g., pentatonicism), how is this evident in the melody or harmony?

## (iii) Form and Structure

- **Analysis:**
    - Provide a detailed structural analysis of this piece. (e.g., "The piece is in Binary Form: Section A (bars 1-16) in the tonic key, Section B (bars 17-32) modulating to the dominant and returning to the tonic.")
    - Label the main sections and themes (e.g., Introduction, Theme A, Bridge, Theme B, Development, Recapitulation, Coda).
    - How does the composer use melodic material? Is there repetition, sequence, or imitation?
    - Identify any clear cadence points that define the end of a phrase or section.
- **Context (for movements of larger works):**
    - What is the larger work from which this movement is taken? (e.g., Sonata No. X, Suite No. Y)
    - What is the position of this movement within that work? (e.g., 1st movement, 3rd movement)
    - What is the typical structure of the larger work (e.g., a sonata typically has 3-4 movements in a fast-slow-dance-fast pattern)?
    - How does the character of this movement contrast with the other movements?

# Part 2: Questions Based on General Knowledge & Context

## (i) Period, Style, and Composer

- **Period and Style:**
    - To which musical period does this piece belong (e.g., Baroque, Classical, Romantic, 20th Century)?
    - Describe three stylistic features of this period that are evident in `[Piece Title]`.
    - How is this piece typical of the style of its time? Are there any atypical or innovative features?
- **Composer:**
    - What are the dates of the composer, `[Composer]`?
    - What nationality were they, and with which musical institutions/cities are they most associated?
    - Name two other important works by this composer (they can be for any instrument or ensemble).
    - Who were their major influences, and which composers did they influence in turn?
    - What was the composer's primary role? (e.g., court musician, church organist, virtuoso performer, freelance composer)
- **Broader Context:**
    - Name one prominent orchestral composer from the same period and a work they wrote.
    - Name one prominent vocal/choral composer from the same period and a work they wrote.
    - What major historical or cultural events were happening during this period that might have influenced the arts?
